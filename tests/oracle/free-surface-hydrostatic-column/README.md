# A liquid column at rest under gravity

A column 1 m tall, four cells (Δx = 25 mm) wide between no-slip walls and one cell deep (periodic), holds liquid (ρ = 1000 kg/m³)
up to h = 0.5 m under gravity g = 9.81 m/s², gas above. At rest the pressure is hydrostatic,
p = ρ g (h − z), the surface stays where it is and nothing moves.

**Measures:** the gauge pressure at the lowest cell centre, ρ g (h − Δx/2) = 4782.4 Pa; the liquid's
upper extent at the end (the face above the last cell at least half full), h; the largest speed at
the end (zero); the relative mass error (zero).

**Run:** the lattice gravity is 10⁻⁴ (Δt = 5.0482e-04 s), so the density grows by 0.6 % down the
column and the compressed column stands 0.3 % (0.06 cells) lower than h; the walls damp the start-up oscillation of the column (a column periodic across, with nothing but the bulk viscosity to damp it, still rings after 4000 steps); τ = 0.6 (a viscosity of
41.27 Pa·s, which only sets how fast the start-up waves die); 4000 steps, 2.019 s, about
60 crossings of the column by sound.
