# Meshing test corpus

Generated by `cargo xtask corpus`; do not edit. The parts are our own: SolidCraft recipes in
`tests/corpus/<name>/recipe.json` and analytic shapes generated in code
(`crates/testkit/src/corpus.rs`). Each run rebuilds every part, tessellates it at a chordal
tolerance of 1e-3 of its bounding-box diagonal and runs every stage on it. Times are wall
clock on the machine that ran it; every other column is deterministic on one platform.

## Regression gate

Each part's results are frozen in `tests/corpus/<name>/fixture.json` (an analytic shape's
directory holds only its fixture), in the oracle's format (docs/verification.md):

- `stages`: every stage's outcome, compared exactly: `ok` (with the method, for the hex stage),
  `no: <kind>` when the mesher refuses the part (a limit of the method today, such as no
  structured hex method fitting) or `error: <kind>`. A part that starts or stops meshing is a
  change either way.
- `values`: the measures. Counts (tetrahedra, slivers, Steiner points, hexes, boundary vertices,
  genus) have a band of 0; reals (volumes, dihedral angles, radius ratios, scaled Jacobians) a
  band of 10⁻⁹ of their size plus 10⁻¹², and a relative difference (the enclosed volume against
  the exact one) 10⁻⁹. On one platform a run is deterministic bit for bit; the bands absorb only
  last-digit differences of the system maths library. Times are never frozen.
- `platforms`: each platform the fixture was blessed on, with what it measures differently.
  The tessellation of curved faces differs in its last bits between macOS aarch64 and Linux
  x86_64 (`cross-cylinders` gets a triangle more on Linux), and the tet mesher turns that into
  a different mesh (`bent-tube` has 25167 tetrahedra on macOS and 28107 on Linux, the smallest
  dihedral angle of `bolted-assembly` is 13.6° on one and 28.2° on the other); on curved parts
  the octree's scaled Jacobians and volume move too, by up to 1%. A band wide enough for both
  would gate nothing, so each platform is checked against its own results. Every stage's
  outcome and the structured hex stage agree across both within the bands, and so do about half
  of the parts in full (their `platforms` entries are empty).

Every tet mesh of the quality stage is also checked on the test side
(`crates/testkit/src/validity.rs`), independently of the mesher's gate: every tetrahedron
positive; every face held by one tetrahedron (boundary) or two on opposite sides (inside); the
boundary faces a closed, consistently oriented 2-manifold without a pinched vertex; no
tetrahedron joined to the rest by a vertex or an edge only, and one face-connected piece per
solid; the mesher's list of boundary faces equal to the faces held once. The column `mesh
validity` shows the result.

`cargo xtask corpus` and the heavy test `corpus_matches_its_fixtures` (CI's heavy job, on Linux
x86_64) fail when a part moves from its fixture, has none, or breaks an invariant.

### Blessing

When a change moves a part on purpose, bless it and say in the pull request what moved and why
that is right (more tetrahedra from a finer size field, a part that now meshes with the hex
sweep). A part that stops meshing or meshes worse is a regression until shown otherwise. A
broken invariant is a bug, never a fixture value: blessing does not hide it, and the run fails
until the mesher is fixed.

Bless on both platforms, macOS first, then Linux x86_64 in Docker:

```
cargo xtask corpus --bless <name>          # or --bless all; --jobs 4 meshes four parts at once
docker run --rm --platform linux/amd64 -v "$PWD":/src -w /src -e CARGO_TARGET_DIR=/target \
  ubuntu:24.04 bash -c 'apt-get update -qq && apt-get install -y -qq build-essential curl pkg-config git \
  && curl -sSf https://sh.rustup.rs | sh -s -- -y --profile minimal && . ~/.cargo/env \
  && cargo run --release -j4 -p fieldcraft-testkit --bin corpus -- --bless <name> --jobs 4 --no-write'
```

A bless on a platform the fixture already records rewrites the fixture from that platform alone
when its results moved (the other platforms' results are stale then, and the run there fails
until it is blessed there too); a bless on a platform the fixture does not record yet adds what
it measures differently. Blessing a part whose results did not move changes nothing.

## Parts

- `bent-tube`: A 10 mm tube with a 1 mm wall, bent through R30: a thin-walled sweep along a line-arc-line path.
- `bolted-assembly`: A base plate with a clamp block component placed twice, once turned 90 degrees: bodies placed by component transforms.
- `cantilever-i-beam`: The 200 mm I-beam of `i-beam` lying along X with its web upright (Z up): a sweep along a horizontal axis, and the cantilever of the README demo.
- `cooling-enclosure`: A 40 x 44 mm PCB, 2 mm thick, standing upright with a 16 x 6 x 12 mm chip on its face, as two bodies: the natural-convection cooling sample's parts (docs/examples.md).
- `cooling-heat-sink`: A 40 x 40 mm heat sink, a 3 mm base with six 1.5 mm fins 18 mm tall at a 6 mm pitch: thin, closely spaced fins on a plate (the forced-convection cooling sample, docs/examples.md).
- `cross-cylinders`: Two orthogonal R10 cylinders joined: equal radii, so the intersection curves cross at two singular points.
- `filleted-bracket`: An 8 mm L-bracket with an R6 inside fillet and a hole through each leg: a blend beside holes.
- `filleted-bracket-blocks`: The filleted bracket split into 30 cells with SolidCraft's Split Body (three slabs across its width, the corner halved along its diagonal, an O-grid of four cells round each hole) so that every cell is a mapped block: conforming multi-block hex meshing.
- `i-beam`: A 200 mm I-beam with 3 mm flanges and a 2 mm web: a long sweepable prism with thin walls.
- `loft-transition`: A 40 mm square lofted to an R15 circle 50 mm above: a ruled free-form surface between unlike sections.
- `perforated-plate`: A 3 mm plate perforated by a 6 x 4 grid of 6 mm holes: genus 24 in a thin sheet.
- `pinhole-block`: A 50 mm block drilled through by sixteen 0.6 mm pinholes: features 80 times smaller than the part, genus 16.
- `rounded-box`: A 40 x 30 x 20 box with every edge rounded R3: cylinder and sphere blends meeting at the corners.
- `shallow-groove`: A block with a 0.5 mm deep groove cut by an R30 cylinder: groove walls 10 degrees off the top face.
- `sharp-wedge`: A wedge whose faces meet at under 3 degrees: a sharp edge with a needle-thin cross-section.
- `sphere-on-plate`: A sphere sunk 0.1 mm into a plate: the faces meet at under 6 degrees around a 2 mm circle.
- `spline-vase`: A vase revolved from a spline profile: doubly curved faces of varying curvature around a closed axis.
- `stepped-block`: Three stacked blocks with a slot cut through the base: axis-aligned steps for polycube and octree meshers.
- `thin-ribs`: A 4 mm plate carrying five 0.8 mm ribs 20 mm tall: thin walls standing on a thicker base.
- `thin-wall-enclosure`: An 80 x 60 x 40 box shelled to 1 mm walls, open on top: thin walls over a large span.
- `analytic-sphere`: Sphere r 20: curvature everywhere, no edges.
- `analytic-torus`: Torus 30/8: genus 1 with doubly curved faces and no edges.
- `analytic-cylinder`: Cylinder r 10, h 40: the round bar of beam and torsion checks.
- `analytic-pipe`: Thick-walled pipe 20/30, length 60: the Lamé cylinder.
- `analytic-plate-hole`: Plate 200 x 100 x 5 with a 20 mm hole: the Kirsch plate.
